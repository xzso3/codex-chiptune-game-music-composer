# Chiptune-inspired Grammar Reference

This reference describes the **musical language** used by the skill. It is not a hardware-accuracy guide.

## Identity layers

A cue reads as chiptune-inspired when several signals reinforce each other. Do not rely on a square wave alone.

Core signals:
- concise, high-recognition melodic motif;
- repetition with small controlled variation;
- pulse/square-forward lead identity;
- broken-chord or rapid-arpeggio harmonic motion;
- active bass with clear scale/chord function;
- pattern-oriented drums and subdivisions;
- digital/noise-like attacks;
- compact forms designed to loop;
- limited role count with each role remaining perceptually clear.

## Motif rules

Prefer 4-8 note cells with a distinct contour. Good transformations:
- sequence by scale degree;
- last-note/ending substitution;
- octave displacement;
- rhythmic compression/expansion;
- call/response fragment;
- ornament insertion;
- occasional rest insertion.

Avoid completely unrelated melody every bar. Chiptune identity benefits from recognizability and reuse.

## Harmony

The system may use ordinary tonal/modal harmony; the chiptune character often comes from **how** harmony is presented:
- broken triads;
- rapid 1-3-5 / 1-5-3 cycles;
- root/fifth implication;
- bass + melody jointly supplying chord tones;
- pedal tones;
- modal loops.

A modern chiptune-inspired cue may also use sustained pads or richer harmony. Authentic voice scarcity is not a constraint.

## Bass

Bass should contribute groove rather than act only as a whole-note root bed.

Useful cells:
- root repetitions;
- root-fifth movement;
- octave pulse;
- approach-note movement when the style pack supports it;
- lock with kick/backbeat for Rock/Electronic hybrids.

## Arpeggio

Arp density is a primary identity/fatigue control.

High density:
- strong retro/chiptune signal;
- good for action/electronic energy;
- fatigue risk if continuous for an entire long loop.

Low density:
- leaves space for lead and ambience;
- useful in town/exploration/ambient contexts.

Treat arp as an arrangement role, not a mandatory always-on effect.

## Timbre vocabulary

Suggested, not exclusive:
- square/pulse lead;
- pulse counterline;
- triangle-like bass;
- digital pluck;
- wavetable/FM-like accent;
- noise percussion;
- bitcrushed or hybrid modern drums;
- digital pad/atmosphere.

## Chiptune strength vs modern strength

These are independent dimensions.

Example:
- high chiptune strength + high modern strength = clearly chip-authored melody/arp/bass language rendered with contemporary drums, stereo effects and layering.
- high chiptune strength + low modern strength = more stripped, retro-forward presentation.
- low chiptune strength + high modern strength = secondary chiptune coloration around a mostly modern cue; use with caution for this skill.

## Anti-patterns

- ordinary pop/EDM composition with only a square preset swapped in;
- uninterrupted 16th-note arp on every cue regardless of context;
- too many independent lead roles competing in the same register;
- generating every bar independently;
- terminal endings that make a game loop obviously restart;
- confusing historical hardware limitations with desired aesthetic identity.
